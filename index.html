Orient

Find your way, quietly.

What Orient is

Orient is a small, calm search engine. You type what you’re curious about, it looks in many places at once, and it brings everything back as one clean list. No accounts, no ads, no feed trying to keep you here.

How it works

Pick Web for a broad look, Wiki for encyclopedias and reference, or Archive for books, old documents, video and audio. Orientall does all of that together. Orient mixes what it finds, removes duplicates, and lets you load more whenever you want to go deeper.

Your privacy, honestly

Orient has no accounts, no server of its own, and it doesn’t keep a search history. Your search goes straight from your browser to the open services that supply the results, without cookies or a referrer, and those services see it the way any website sees a visitor. On Web searches, Google also shows its own results in a separate box inside Orient and handles that search the way it normally does. This page is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may keep ordinary visit logs. The only thing Orient remembers on your device is a small preference, like whether results open in the same tab.

Everywhere you are

Orient fits phones, tablets and big screens. On a computer, press / to jump to the search box, and in your browser’s settings you can make Orient your default search, so every search you type in the address bar starts here.

Good to know

Orient is young, and it only knows what its sources know. Some searches will find less than a giant search engine would, and now and then a source may be busy. If a search comes back thin, try another type or different words.

Who made it

Orient was made by Pradhyum Bajpai, with a simple wish: that searching should feel quiet, quick and respectful of the person doing it. Thank you for using it.
